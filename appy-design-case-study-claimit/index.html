Product Overview

Claimit turns everyday spending into everyday savings.

Claimit is a hyper-local rewards platform where customers discover nearby businesses, scan bills, earn reward points, receive cashback, and use points as discounts on future purchases.

100%Reward point value on eligible bills
1%Cashback credited to wallet
5 kmHyper-local offer radius
FreeDownload, join, earn, and save

Project Goals

Make “Shop → Scan → Earn → Enjoy” simple to understand.

The design goal was to make Claimit’s reward journey feel clear, trustworthy, and repeatable for customers while supporting local business discovery.

01
Explain the value fast

Communicate rewards, discounts, and 1% cashback without confusion.

02
Guide the core habit

Help users shop normally, scan bills, earn points, and redeem later.

03
Improve local discovery

Surface nearby deals, brand deals, promo content, and local services.

04
Support both zones

Make Reward Zone earning and Redeem Zone discounts easy to compare.

Card Sorting

The product follows a familiar mental model.

We used card sorting to shape navigation around the way people naturally understand ClaimIt.

Why card sorting?

ClaimIt combines deals, rewards, redemption, wallet activity, notifications, and account tools. Card sorting helped us understand how users naturally group this content and which labels felt clearest before finalizing the navigation.

How we conducted it

We conducted card-sorting sessions with real users from the key audience groups. Participants organized feature cards into categories, named the groups in their own words, and explained where they expected to find each function.

What we learned

Users consistently separated discovery, reward activity, notifications, and profile settings. These patterns directly informed the final information architecture.

Paper Sketches

Early ideas were tested with fast hand sketches.

Before moving to digital wireframes, we explored the core ClaimIt screens on paper to validate structure, navigation, and reward actions quickly.

Why Paper Sketches?

Paper sketches helped us focus on layout, hierarchy, and user decisions before spending time on visual styling. This made it easier to compare ideas, remove weak flows, and align the team around the simplest experience.

01
Started with key tasks

We mapped the main actions users need: discover nearby deals, scan bills, redeem offers, and track cashback.

02
Explored screen structure

Each sketch tested where search, categories, offer cards, wallet balance, and redemption details should appear.

03
Validated before wireframes

The strongest layouts were selected and refined into digital wireframes with clearer spacing, states, and navigation.

Digital Wireframes

Low-fidelity screens defined the product structure.

The selected paper sketches were converted into digital wireframes to refine hierarchy, spacing, navigation, and key reward states before visual design.

Why Digital Wireframes?

Digital wireframes helped us turn rough ideas into a clearer product blueprint. We used them to test layout consistency, check screen transitions, and make sure the core reward journey was simple before adding color and branding.

01
Converted sketches into screens

Home, deals, offer detail, redemption success, and wallet were structured as the core user journey.

02
Refined hierarchy and spacing

Search, categories, offer cards, CTA placement, and bottom navigation were adjusted for better scanning.

03
Prepared for visual design

The wireframes became the foundation for UI styling, design system components, and prototype screens.

Visual Design

One visual language connects every product moment.

Strong hierarchy, rounded surfaces, clear actions, and consistent reward states support discovery, earning, and redemption.

Design System Components

Reusable components made the product faster and more consistent.

The ClaimIt component library defined repeatable UI patterns for buttons, chips, headers, cards, navigation, filters, icons, and featured zones.

Why Components?

Components turn repeated interface patterns into reusable building blocks. They keep screens visually consistent, reduce redesign work, and make every new feature easier to assemble.

01
Why it is important

Buttons, cards, tabs, headers, and filters behave the same across discovery, redemption, wallet, and profile flows.

02
How we used it in Figma

We created master components with variants for active, inactive, primary, secondary, selected, and default states.

03
Design to handoff

Reusable components helped developers inspect spacing, states, colors, and usage rules from one organized source.

Color & Typography

Visual foundations created a clear and consistent product language.

Color, typography, spacing, and mobile grid rules were documented so every screen could feel aligned, readable, and easy to scale.

Why Foundations?

Foundations define the visual rules behind every interface decision. They make the product feel intentional instead of screen-by-screen, and they give the team a shared reference for future updates.

01
Color system

Blue builds trust and anchors actions, yellow highlights rewards, and neutral colors keep content readable.

02
Typography scale

Poppins was used with clear heading and body sizes so titles, labels, and descriptions stay easy to scan.

03
Spacing and grid

A consistent mobile spacing system helped align cards, buttons, lists, and screen layouts across the app.

Accessibility

Accessibility review focused on clarity and confidence.

We checked the core screens for readable labels, visible feedback, comfortable tap areas, and alternate input options.

Why accessibility mattered

Claimit depends on quick actions in real shopping contexts. Users need to understand what to tap, what changed, and what happens next.
